How the Elo rating works

Every ranked game in Blockmate changes your Elo rating, the same system chess has used for decades to rank players. It measures your level against everyone else's, and it rewards upsets.

Where Elo comes from

The system was designed by Arpad Elo, a physics professor and chess master, and adopted by the United States Chess Federation in 1960 and by the World Chess Federation (FIDE) in 1970. Today it is used in chess, in many online games and even in sports rankings.

The idea in one sentence

Before each game, the system predicts how likely you are to win from the difference between the two ratings. If you do better than predicted, you gain points; if you do worse, you lose them.

The formula

Your expected score against an opponent is E = 1 / (1 + 10(Ropponent − Ryou) / 400). After the game, your new rating is your old rating plus K × (result − E), where the result is 1 for a win, 0.5 for a draw and 0 for a loss. Blockmate uses K = 32.

What does not change your Elo

Practice games, games against a friend and the daily challenge never change your rating. Only ranked games against bots and online ranked matches do.

FAQ

Why did I lose more points than I won?

Because your opponent was rated lower than you: the system expected you to win, so a loss costs more.

Can my rating go below zero?

No. Blockmate keeps every rating at 100 or more.
