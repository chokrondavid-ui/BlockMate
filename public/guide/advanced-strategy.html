Advanced Blockmate strategy

Once you know the rules, Blockmate becomes a game of planning. Strong players don't just look for the biggest clear: they think about what their move leaves for the opponent. Here are the ideas that separate a Gold player from a Master.

1. Every move is also a gift

The two pieces you don't play become your rival's hand. Before you place, ask two questions: which of my three pieces scores best for me, and which two pieces are worst for my opponent on the board I'm about to leave? Often the second question matters more. Keeping an awkward 3×3 square or an L for your rival on a crowded board can win the game on its own.

2. Read the next choices

The "next choices" row shows exactly what your opponent will hold. If they will get a long 1×5 bar and you leave a row with five free cells in a line, you have just handed them a clear. Close those gaps, or leave them only when the piece they get can't fill them.

3. Two-line setups

One line scores 10, two lines at once score 30, three score 60. The jump is huge, so it often pays to build two almost-full lines that cross the same empty cells, then finish both with one piece. The danger: a half-built double is a gift if your rival can finish it first. Build doubles where only shapes you hold can complete them.

4. Protect the combo

Clearing on consecutive turns raises your multiplier: ×1.25, then ×1.5, then ×1.75. A small single clear that keeps the combo alive is worth more than it looks, because it boosts the big clear you are preparing. When your combo is high, keep at least one "easy" line ready for the next turn.

5. Keep the centre open

Big pieces (3×3 squares, long bars, large L shapes) need open space. Fill the edges and corners first with small pieces, and keep the middle of the board breathing. A board with a clean centre gives you options; a fragmented one gives you none.

6. Play for the block in the endgame

When the board is nearly full, scoring gets hard and blocking becomes the real game. If your rival can't place any of their pieces, the game ends and you get +25. Look at their next choices and leave a board where none of them fit. Count it out: when you are ahead, a block wins; when you are behind, you need the clears more than the block.

7. Manage the clock

In timed games, don't spend your time on easy moves. Play the obvious opening placements quickly and save your minutes for the crowded middle game and the endgame, where one mistake decides the result.

FAQ

What is the most common mistake?

Leaving a line one piece away from being cleared, with that exact piece in the rival's next choices.

How do I train?

Play the Master bot in Practice with a score target of 200, and play the daily challenge every day to learn which shapes fit together.
